# Kawal Asap manifesto

## Why we build

People affected by fires and haze need information they can understand and
check. Residents need to know what a source shows, how recent it is, what is
uncertain, and where to find reviewed guidance. Journalists and the public need
records they can question, not claims they must accept on trust.

We are building Kawal Asap to serve these needs in Indonesia and help people
understand impacts across borders. Our purpose is to help people act, protect
reviewable evidence, and support public scrutiny of companies and government.

## Our commitments

### 1. Put affected people first

Build for residents, not only for technical users. Use clear Bahasa Indonesia,
accessible tables, and pages that work on small screens. Give public information
without requiring an account. Favor useful, reliable information over visual
effects.

Health and safety guidance must have sources, a review date, and review by a
qualified person. We do not replace local emergency services or provide
personal medical advice.

### 2. Show what the evidence can support

A satellite detection is an observation, not a confirmed fire or a count of
separate fires. A model is not a measurement on the ground. A missing feed is
not evidence that there are no fires.

Show sources, observation times, methods, and limits. Make stale, partial, and
unavailable data clear. Keep observations, submitted accounts, and public
claims distinct. Approval in a private review is not permission to publish.
Correct errors openly.

### 3. Protect people while preserving evidence

Photos, videos, and testimony can help explain conditions that would otherwise
be hard to check. Collect them with clear consent, purpose, and reuse terms.
Do not expose a person or a precise location merely because a file was submitted.

Keep private originals separate from reviewed public copies. Preserve source
context and correction history under agreed retention rules. Provide a safe
process for redaction, erasure, and withdrawal. Do not promise to preserve
everything forever or to recall copies that others have already obtained.

### 4. Support accountability without automatic blame

Land and concession records can help people ask who controls an affected area.
Before making a match public, check the source, date, boundaries, company
identity, and reuse rights. Show gaps, overlaps, and uncertainty.
Distinguish land ownership from a right to use land.

A detection inside a concession does not prove that its operator caused a fire.
A regional detection count is not, by itself, a verdict on government
performance. Reports of deleted content or platform restrictions also need
evidence; reduced reach alone does not prove deliberate suppression.

### 5. Make checked information useful to others

Help residents, journalists, researchers, and public institutions trace claims
to their sources. Share reviewed information through stable pages with clear
dates and reuse terms. Support useful briefings across borders without making
claims stronger in translation.

Learn from existing efforts, including [AmukApi](https://amukapi.santaralabs.ai/),
and seek cooperation where it helps people. A reference is not a partnership
or an endorsement of every method. Check rights before reusing code or data.

### 6. Build in the open

Keep the code, decisions, methods, known limits, and remaining work open to
inspection. Welcome corrections and contributions from people with different
skills. Open code does not make private records public, and the code's MIT
license does not replace the rights attached to data or media.

## Where we stand

This manifesto states our purpose and principles, not a claim that every
workflow is ready. The current preview uses synthetic data. Live satellite
ingestion and public report publication are not yet implemented. Release checks
must pass before real data is made public. See the [README](README.md) for
implementation status and [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/kawaltech/kawal.id/issues)
for remaining work.

## Help build it

We welcome help with engineering, accessibility, testing, documentation,
geospatial methods, journalism, and reviewed fire-and-haze guidance. Read
[CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), discuss one bounded task, and agree ownership
before starting. Use the [security policy](SECURITY.md) for private reports of
vulnerabilities. Do not put personal data or private evidence in public issues.

**Help people. Protect sources. Make the evidence open to scrutiny.**
